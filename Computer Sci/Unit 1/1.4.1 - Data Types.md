## Twos Complement 

For Two's Complement all negative numbers always start with one as the MSB (Most Significant Bit) represents a negative value. 

For Example with 8 Bits the MSB would be -128

Let's say we have a number 117:

Since the number is positive the MSB would be 0
Then we can convert from Denary to Binary as Normal 

| -128 | 64  | 32  | 16  | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   |
| ---- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0    | 1   | 1   | 1   | 0   | 1   | 0   | 1   |

64 +32 +4 +1 = 117


Now for - 117
The MSB would be 1
Then we go to Denary to Binary as Normal however rather than trying not to go over the value we try to keep the number as negative as we can

| -128 | 64  | 32  | 16  | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   |
| ---- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1    | 0   | 0   | 0   | 1   | 0   | 1   | 1   |


-128 +8 +2 = -117


As you can see there is a pattern between both of these numbers 

To easily get the negative number of any number using twos complement we can use this simple method: 

1.  Write out the positive number 
2. Staring from the LSB (The Least significant bit) copy each of the digits as they appear 
3. From this point on, swap every digit from 1 to 0 and 0 to 1 LSB


For example the number 12 and -12 

| -128 | 64  | 32  | 16  | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   |
| ---- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0    | 0   | 0   | 0   | 1   | 1   | 0   | 0   |

^-- This is the binary for 12 

So using this method we get  for step 1 and step 2


| -128 | 64  | 32  | 16  | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   |
| ---- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -    | -   | -   | -   | -   | 1   | 0   | 0   |


And finally 

| -128 | 64  | 32  | 16  | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   |
| ---- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1    | 1   | 1   | 1   | 0   | 1   | 0   | 0   |

## Floating Point Binary

When storing numbers with a fractional component such as 6.5 we adjust the weights on the Binary to have fractions by halving each time going to the right

For example 

| -16 | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   | .1/2 | 1/4 | 1/8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---- | --- | --- |
| 0   | 0   | 1   | 1   | 0   | 1    | 0   | 0   |

So 6.5 would be 0011 0100

And 3.75 would be 0001 1110

| -16 | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   | .1/2 | 1/4 | 1/8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---- | --- | --- |
| 0   | 0   | 0   | 1   | 1   | 1    | 1   | 0   |
We can use twos complement to get negatives as well 

This whole thing is known as fixed point binary, and we lose some the range of numbers we can store as you can see the largest number that we can store is 15.875.

However, there are also numbers that we can store accurately with this format such as 1/3.


So increase accuracy, we can change the way we use the binary point.

This means that we can change where the number starts with  the 8 bits

So in other words the binary point is floating up and down the number line.

As we move the binary point left and right we can sacrifice range for accuracy and sacrifice accuracy for range.

This is called floating point binary.


However, We actually need to store where the binary point is on the number line and in order to store this.

We can do this by splitting the bits into two parts.

The Mantissa : the number itself 
The Exponent : The position of the binary point in the number.

A 32 bit, single precision number uses 24 bits for the mantissa and 8 for the exponent , giving 24 bits of precision.

In the exam, the number of bits used for the mantissa and exponent will always be stated.


If we look at number : 01100 011

The Mantissa: 5 bits (01100)
The Exponent: 3 bits (011)

The Binary point always starts after the MSB, between the first two digits in the mantissa - but that is not where it ends.


So let's take a look at the Exponent

| -4  | 2   | 1   |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0   | 1   | 1   |

There the Exponent = 3 

This means that we move the Binary Point three places to the right. (Its the right because the exponent is positive

| -1  | 1/2 | 1/4 | 1/8 | .1/16 |
