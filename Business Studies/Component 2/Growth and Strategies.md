
## Ansoff's Matrix 

The Ansoff Growth Matrix is another marketing planning tool that helps a business determine its product and market growth strategy.

The output from the Ansoff product/matrix matrix is a series of  suggested growth strategies  which set the direction for the business strategy.

Ansoff looked at the degree of risk and potential for reward  from different strategic options.

He recognized that as a firm moved away from that it knows best i.e. its current product and current market the degree of risk increased 

However trying to just sell more of an existing product in an existing market is unlikely to being out substantial growth opportunities.

### Market Penetration 
This is a growth strategy that focuses upon how a business can increase sales within their existing market with their current products.

Selling more of an existing product to an existing market.

Low risk strategy but limited potential rewards.

These are some methods 
- Encouraging new users of your products 
- Targeting competitor sales
- Increase usage of existing usage.
  
Also this can be done by using the 4 P's of marketing 

For example by manipulating P - Price  to a lower amount might drive sales up which is counted as growth however  Revenue might go up or down depending on the products PED  (See - [Markets](</Business Studies/Component 1 - Opportunities and Finance/Markets.md#PED - (Price Elasticity of Demand)>))


Analysis - 
- How will competitors react?
- Are the actions sustainable?
- Is the market already saturated?
-  if multi product will the outcome just be cannibalization?
  
  
  Examples: 
  
  Nestle wanted to increase the number of people eating shredded wheat, they did this by using a famous sportsperson advertise it  --> this is an example of Increase usage of existing usage.

Kellogg's also used Increase usage of existing usage in their product Special K by advertising it as the best cereal to lose weight

### Market Development 
This growth strategy involves identifying new markets for a firm's existing products.

Risk associated with lack of knowledge of customers (may be new customer type or new location)

This could be done via: 

- Identifying new geographical markets
  -  Some risk as every country's population hold different tastes, interests and needs.
  - Products  adapted for sale in new markets = costs?
  - Markets campaigns might also require adjustment 
- Finding new customers and uses for existing products 


Examples include: 
Gin - An example of Finding new customers --> Went from Elderly people to younger people 

Lucozade Sport  - An example of Finding new customers --> went from hospital patents to sports people 

Analysis - 
-  Will the product translate/ be accepted abroad?
- Does the business understand the target market?
- How accurate is the market analysis?
- Could current customers be alienated?


### Product Development
Selling new and better products to existing customers

Medium risk - comes from not knowing the products, high R&D costs and competitors' reactions. (This changes based on much the business makes when dealing with monetary risk for example £1000 is a alot to me but is not alot to Elon Musk therefore the riskier for me)

Investment in new products takes time and money as R&D is a lengthy process.

Many new ideas fail in the development phase and never reach the customer. This is a sunk cost that cannot be
recovered.

Market research is essential to allow a firm to monitor changing consumer tastes and fashions and to identify new
technologies and trends.

How?

- Launch substantially improved version of existing products
- Introduce complementary products
- Extension strategies
- New product innovations

Analysis –

- Risk of cannibalization
- Cost if R&D
- May shorten product life cycle of existing products
- Could brand image be damaged?
### Diversification

Selling new products to new markets

High risk strategy as 2 elements are unknown - the market and the
product but also greatest potential for reward

Diversification does allow a business to spread risk by avoiding only
focusing upon their existing products and markets in which sales might
be in decline.

How?
- R&D into new products and market research into new markets
- Acquisitions of other businesses

Analysis

- Relies on heavy investment
- Will cultural differences arise?
- Will brand name be strengthened or diluted?

For example Samsung with their phones, frigidness, TV, tablets, The Burj Khalifa
