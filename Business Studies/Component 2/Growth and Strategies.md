
## Ansoff's Matrix 

The Ansoff Growth Matrix is another marketing planning tool that helps a business determine its product and market growth strategy.

The output from the Ansoff product/matrix matrix is a series of  suggested growth strategies  which set the direction for the business strategy.

Ansoff looked at the degree of risk and potential for reward  from different strategic options.

He recognized that as a firm moved away from that it knows best i.e. its current product and current market the degree of risk increased 

However trying to just sell more of an existing product in an existing market is unlikely to being out substantial growth opportunities.

## Outsourcing 

The delegation of one or more business processes to an **external provider**, who then owns, manages and administers the selected processes to an agreed standard "(CIPD)"

Outsourcing is a business practice used by companies to reduce costs or improve efficiency by transferring jobs or activities to tan externally contracted third party for a significant period of time.

Firms use outsourcing as a means of freeing them up to **focus upon their core business**. 

The outsourcing company is not directly employed by the business and jobs might be lost as employment move overseas (off-shoring).

The intention is that activities are outsources to specialists who can do tasks more effectively 
and therefore cheaper, saving the business money.

### Advantages and Disadvantages

#### Advantages

Significantly reduced staffing costs.
Well-trained staff provided by the outsourcing company will reduce HRM costs such as recruitment and training

Existing workload and stress levels reduced, which is very important if av business if operating at near full capacity.

Less investment risk. Instead of investing in a new production facilities, lets the outside supplier take the risk of investing. (Investment Appraislal)

Capital needs to be reduced - because there is less investment , there is less need ti raise finance.

Lower Costs increase profits giving more capital for R&D, so speeding up the development of new product.

Ability to use a specialist business who mat be able to produce at higher quality or more efficiency.Allows the business to specialize/concentrate on other areas. 


#### Disadvantages

Potential of poor customer service, with the potential for communication to be made difficult.

Existing employees may feel demotivated if they feel their job is at risk.

Quality cannot be guaranteed.

More difficult to implement JIT (just in time) systems.

Longer communication.


## Rationalization

Rationalization is the process of deliberately downsizing a business to enable to improve operational control.

Firms use this to :

- To mitigate the effects of Diseconomies of scale 
- Getting rid of excess stock and resources (This inefficiency can cost the business money) 


By the way this is also known as Retrenchment 

### What are the Causes of Retrenchment 

Retrenchment normally arises from decisions to change strategic direction, which in turn usually happens because of one or more of the following: 

- New Leadership (Normally a new CEO)
- Excessively-high costs and low profitability (or unsustainable losses)
- Low ROCE (Return on Capital Employed)
- Excessively high gearing (leading to cash flow problems)
- Loss of Market Share
- A failed takeover or merger
- Economic downturn


### How is it done?

- Closure of unprofitable branches 
- Relocating production facilities to cut costs 
- Removing less profitable products from sale
- Automation, Capital Intensive

*"Capital Intensive refers to having machines do the work of humans"*


### What are the Problems with Rationalization?

- The nature of rationalization via downsizing inevitably means that there will be **job losses**.
- Job losses can attract trade union action, negative media attention and stakeholder protests within the local community.
- Job insecurity  concerns for remaining employees. → this could also affect motivation (Via Maslow's Theory of hierarchy of needs)
- Employees might be uncertain about the future direction of the company and as a result might look for other work leading to a rise in labour turnover.

It's important to existing businesses because it allows them to help define their strategy and better position themselves in their markets

### Barriers to Entry 

These are factors that **prevent new firms from entering** a market.

High barriers to entry make a market less competitive and allows existing forms to charge higher prices and make monopoly profits as consumer choice is restricted though a lack of alternatives.

If new firms can enter a market easily then the market is high competitive and prices tend to be low as consumers can easily switch to substitutes.

Tesco is an example of a very powerful buyer who are able to exert massive power over their suppliers due to their huge purchasing economies of scale.

## Investment Appraisal 

This is the process of analysing whether investment projects are worthwhile 

We look at three different methods of calculating which investment is best, financially 

- Payback Period, The time it takes for a project to repay its initial investment → this is measured in time (Years, Months)
  
- Average Rate of Return, Looks at the total accounting return for a project to see if it meets the target return → this is measured in % return

- Discounted Cash Flow (Or Net Present Value), Net present value calcs the monetary value now of the project's future cashflows → Measured in Monetary value £


### Net Cash Flow

Net Cash Flow (NCF) is a measure of the total amount of cash flowing in and out of a business 

For investment appraisal, NCF helps a firm to understand the financial gains to be had from investing in a particular project.

The higher the net cash flow the quicker a project will repay the initial investment.

#### Cumulative Cash Flow

The cumulative cash flow shows the value of the net cash flow added to the cost of the initial investment.  At first this will be negative until the cost of the investment has been repaid.

## Payback Period 

The payback period is **the time** it takes for a project to **repay its initial investment**.

Basically the calcs for this is quite easy all you gotta do is add up until you are about going over the costs then sub that amount from the costs the get the remmain amount needed 
then you use the remaining amount / Total of the next year X 12

This is the formula:

$$\text{Payback Period} = \text{Years before recovery} + \left( \frac{\text{Remaining Amount Needed}}{\text{Total Cash Flow of Next Year}} \times 12 \right)$$


### Benefits of Using Payback Period 

1. Simple and Easy to calculate + easy to understand the results 
2. Focuses on Cash Flows
3. Emphasizes speed of return; goof for markets which change rapidly 
4. Straightforward to compare competing projects.


### Drawbacks of Using Payback Periods

1. Ignores cash flows after the paycheck has been reached 
2. Takes no account of the "time value of money"
3. May encourage short-term thinking 
4. Ignores qualitative aspects of a choice.
5. Does not actually create a final choice for the investment
