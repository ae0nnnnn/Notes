# Special Order


Special orders differ from what a business normally offers 

Customers might ask for products with : 

-  A shorter lead time than normal 
- Or a reduced price
- or a far higher volume 
- Or for an existing product to be modified in some way and for a higher selling price to be received


## Qualitative factors that need to be considered

1. Capacity - has the business have the spare capacity or is the best way to utilize the spare capacity 
2. Labour demands - would the special order be completed in normal hours or would extra hours have to be paid to workers?
3. Liquidity - would they have the short term funds to cope with the order 
4. Future orders - could the special order lead to a more regular orders
5. Existing customers - will the special order upset existing customers who pay a higher price?
6. Product adjustment - would the special order require a product slightly different to the regular product.


## Contribution 

The starting point of any decision whether to accept or reject the order us the calculation of contribution.

Contribution calculates the amount that each item sold will contribute towards the fixed costs if the business and subsequently towards profits once these have been covered.


### Key Formulas 
$$\text{Total Contribution} = \text{Contribution per Unit} \times \text{Units Sold}$$
$$\text{Contribution Per Unit} = \text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost per Unit}$$
$$\text{Profit} = \text{Total Contribution} - \text{Fixed Costs}$$
